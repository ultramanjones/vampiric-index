The Squeeze

What the government permits a single adult to earn, against what it costs that adult to live in Pittsburgh. Both sides are gross monthly income — the expense side is grossed up for federal, FICA, PA and local tax, so the two wings are directly comparable. Single adult, no dependents, 2026. A model built from published averages — not anyone's personal budget.

The right wing is longer than the left on every single row. Not at the end — at the beginning, in the middle, and at the end. There is no step at which the income a person is permitted to earn covers what that step costs.

Row one. Rent alone — one bedroom, Pittsburgh average, nothing else — already requires more income than the level at which SNAP considers a person employed. No lights on yet.

Row three. Rent, utilities, and a phone. That's it. The income needed has already passed the point where an SSDI claim dies.

Stress-tested against the whole rent range, not just one number. At the low end of the published Pittsburgh one-bedroom average ($1,334/mo) this life needs $5,273/mo ($63,281/yr). At the high end ($1,457/mo), $5,435/mo ($65,216/yr). The 400% cliff sits at $5,320/mo ($63,840/yr) — inside that band. Which side of a $570/mo ($6,840/yr) penalty a person lands on is decided by which apartment they happened to rent.

Rows five, seven, and eight are the cruel ones, because the cost on the right is created by the benefit dying on the left. Medicaid ends → a premium appears — minimum $330/mo ($3,960/yr) at what's currently on the marketplace. Cost-sharing ends → deductibles appear. The 400% subsidy ends → that premium doubles. Each blue bar's death is what makes the orange bar beside it longer. It isn't two staircases. It's one machine.

The red is the wedge — money that must be earned and immediately handed over before a single bill is paid. It starts at 13% on the rent row and grows to 20% by the bottom, because the brackets climb with income.

Which is why the orange alone would have lied. Spending $4,270/mo ($51,240/yr) means earning $5,360/mo ($64,320/yr).

And there's an $842/mo ($10,101/yr) dead zone

Cross the cliff at $5,320/mo ($63,840/yr) and the premium jumps ~$570/mo ($6,840/yr). The lost ground isn't recovered until $6,162/mo ($73,941/yr).

Every wage in that $842/mo ($10,101/yr) band leaves less spendable money than $5,320/mo ($63,840/yr) did. At $5,500/mo ($66,000/yr) it's $434/mo worse. At $5,833/mo ($70,000/yr), $213/mo worse.

The band to avoid: $5,320–$6,162/mo ($63,840–$73,941/yr).

This is not a corporate news feed. It is a dated record of the pattern beneath the headline numbers: what the measurement says, what it cannot say, and where the story is being cosmetically improved by aggregation.

The nominal-revenue trap

When a restaurant chain reports positive same-store sales after menu prices rose faster than sales, the headline can describe more dollars while ordinary people buy fewer meals. That is why the Transmission Engine keeps price, transactions, modeled visits, and household-survey behavior separate.

Price-deflated comparable sales can flag that contradiction. POS transaction panels and mobile-device visit panels can test it more directly. Food-security, emergency-buffer, and affordability surveys show what the household is doing underneath it. None alone is the whole picture; the convergence is the evidence.

How to read this site

Household conditions first. Aggregate theater second.

The global map is a provisional country-comparison layer. The U.S. Transmission Engine is different: it follows the path from high-cost revolving debt and thin savings, into missed payments, corporate-credit stress, and damaged everyday demand.

Nonlinear debt redline

On the U.S. page, credit-card APR above 18% is treated as a redline rather than a gentle linear change. The household-safety score also considers revolving-credit volume. The formula, raw source values, dates, and limitations appear in the dashboard data.

Do not mistake a gauge for a prophecy

A single bad number is not a collapse forecast. Meaning comes when household strain, credit stress, and real-economy weakness deteriorate together over more than one release.
